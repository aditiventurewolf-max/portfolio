**Subject:** What you are actually selling is me not being there

Hello,

I have a cat, and I do his grooming myself with a kit I bought, because I gave up.

Grooming is expensive and the care is worse than I would get anywhere else,
especially when I am not standing in the room. We have endless spa services for
humans. For animals, making the animal comfortable is not something I have seen
anyone actually prioritise.

Boarding scares me more. Not the price. I cannot know whether someone will hold
the same line on his safety that I would, and I cannot know what the other
animals there are carrying.

Here is the thing I keep coming back to, and it is why your model is the
interesting one. Grooming, boarding and sitting are readjustable. If a provider
is bad I switch, or I do it myself. The vet is not. When the treatment is poor
you absorb it and mitigate.

So the whole category is a stakeholder agency problem. Their interest is finishing
this appointment to get to the next one. Mine is my animal. Nothing in the
structure aligns those. A marketplace with vetted partners is the only shape that
can fix it, and vetting is therefore the product, not a feature.

I run the Founder's Office at Bounce, an EV mobility platform. Eight workstreams
end to end, no team, no standing budget, including a partner programme negotiated
across four insurers and a hub network I trained on a new service line.

Worth 15 minutes?
