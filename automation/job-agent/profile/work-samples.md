# Work samples, by segment

The work sample is the whole pitch. Everything else in a cold email is a claim.
The sample is the only part that is evidence.

Read this with `resume.md` open. Every artifact below is one she can actually
produce, and each is anchored to something she has already done. Do not invent a
new format that has no anchor.

## The rule that decides whether it lands

Pick the artifact that matches **what the company visibly needs**, not the one
that is easiest to write or the one she likes most. A brilliant content calendar
sent to a company whose problem is unit economics reads as a template.

So diagnose first, from public signals only:

- The last three changelog entries, blog posts, or release notes. What are they
  spending build time on?
- The job posting's own language. What does it repeat? What does it apologise for?
- The launch thread comments, if there is one. Customers complaining in public
  **is** the need statement, handed over free.
- App store or G2 reviews, for anything consumer.
- Who they just hired, and who they conspicuously have not.

Then pick one artifact. One. Three half-artifacts is worse than one finished
thing.

## Segments

### 1. Founder's office, EIR, chief of staff

**Artifact: the unowned-problem memo.**

One page. Name the problem that is visibly nobody's job, from the outside. Say
what it is costing them in a number she can defend. Lay out what she would do in
the first thirty days, week by week, with the one metric that would tell them it
worked.

Anchor: eight concurrent workstreams at Bounce, every one carried from problem
statement to live with no dedicated team and no standing budget. This is
literally the job, done as the application.

Best for: seed to Series B, founder-led, anywhere the posting says "ambiguity"
or "wear many hats".

### 2. Strategy, market entry, business operations

**Artifact: a sizing memo with a capture rate she will defend.**

TAM, SAM, SOM built from signals anyone can see, then the part most people skip:
a defensible capture benchmark with the reasoning shown. State the assumption
that would break it.

Anchor: sized a Rs 49 Cr per month addressable market for Bounce's next city and
derived a 16% capture benchmark from live operating data, replacing an estimate
leadership could not stand behind.

Best for: anyone expanding into a new city, segment or geography. If they just
announced a market, this writes itself.

### 3. GTM, growth, partnerships

**Artifact: channel teardown plus one pilot design.**

The two or three acquisition channels they are not running. For the best one:
rough unit economics, what the pilot costs, how long to a read, and the number
that kills it.

Anchor: built Bounce's first rider-accessories revenue line from zero, validated
on the ground, priced, piloted, scaled to every Bangalore hub and trained the
hub-manager network. Then opened an advertising line by productising the fleet as
geofenced hyperlocal media, and designed a referral engine that turns riders into
a pre-sales channel.

Best for: post-launch companies with distribution as the bottleneck.

### 4. Operations, programme management, supply

**Artifact: bottleneck map plus the AI-assisted rework.**

Draw their process as far as it is visible from outside. Mark where it breaks.
Show the one step she would rebuild and roughly what it gives back.

Anchor: cut manual processing time by about 30% at Scaler AI Labs by
re-engineering delivery workflows with AI-assisted tooling, and built the QA
systems, training modules and assessment frameworks that held the standard.
Managed 15+ vendors.

Best for: services, marketplaces, ops-heavy anything, and any company whose
posting mentions scale or manual work.

### 5. Product, zero to one, AI products

**Artifact: a spec for the feature their product is visibly missing, plus how
she would instrument it.**

Not a wishlist. One feature, the user it is for, the flow, and the two metrics
that say whether it worked. If it is an AI product, include where she thinks the
evals would break.

Anchor: three public agent projects. A semantic content discovery engine on
vector embeddings, a multi-agent market research tool, and a recursive skill
learner where Claude reads its own output and saves what worked as a new skill.
Plus this job agent.

Best for: devtools, AI infra, anything where the founder is technical and will
read a spec properly.

### 6. Partnerships, vendor, business development

**Artifact: a negotiated-terms sketch.**

The three or four partners she would approach, what each one actually wants out
of it, and the commercial shape that makes it work for both. Name the term she
expects to be the sticking point.

Anchor: ran a four-insurer evaluation for a 10,000-rider base and negotiated the
pilot down to commercials, commission structure and hub operating flow. Now live
in-hub.

Best for: insurance, fintech, embedded anything, and companies whose growth is
gated on a counterparty rather than on spend.

### 7. Content, brand, community

**Artifact: ten posts, written. Or a rebrand rationale.**

Ten actual posts, not ten topics. If the problem is positioning rather than
volume, write the rationale instead: what they are saying now, what it should
say, and why, in their own market's language.

Anchor: published in The Daily Brief at Zerodha on India's pet market going from
26M to 42M pets and the trust gap behind 90% of sales still being offline.
Research on the LinkedIn algorithm across 318,842 posts. Brand and UX teardowns
of Naxatra Labs, Stimuler and others. Closed a company-wide scooter rebrand at
Bounce from agency selection through founder sign-off to print.

Best for: D2C, consumer, pet care, education. Anywhere the founder is the account.

### 8. Data, analytics, business analyst

**Artifact: a metric tree plus the one dashboard they are missing.**

The tree from their north star down to what a team could move this week. Then the
single view nobody has built, with the SQL sketched, not just described.

Anchor: SQL, Metabase, Tableau, Power BI, advanced Excel financial modelling,
Python with Pandas and NumPy. Derived the 16% capture benchmark from live
operating data.

Best for: anywhere the posting asks for SQL and the company is past its first
hundred customers.

## Stage modifier

**Pre-seed and seed, team under 15.** The founder reads it themselves, today.
One page, no preamble, and be concrete about week one. They are not hiring a
strategy, they are hiring a pair of hands with judgement.

**Series A to B, team 15 to 60.** There is already a team and a plan. Do not
send a whole strategy, it reads as arrogance. Aim at one visible gap and be
explicit that she is guessing from outside.

**Bigger than that.** A cold approach is a long shot and the sample matters less
than finding the right person. Keep it short, apply properly as well.

## Vertical modifier

- **Consumer and D2C** — trust and retention, not acquisition. Indian consumer
  especially: the offline-to-online trust gap is her published territory.
- **Pet care and education** — she has real market research here. Lead with it.
- **Mobility, logistics, gig** — she works in it now. Use hub economics, rider
  behaviour and fleet operations as the shared language.
- **Devtools and AI infra** — lead with the agents she has shipped. Show the
  eval or instrumentation thinking, not the enthusiasm.
- **Fintech and insurance** — the four-insurer negotiation is the closest thing
  she has to a licence to speak here.

## Length and shape

- 200 to 400 words of substance. It is proof of taste, not a consulting
  deliverable.
- One number she is willing to defend beats five she is not.
- Show the assumption that would break her conclusion. Being specifically wrong
  reads as competence. Being vague reads as nothing.
- Their product, by name, throughout. If the sample would work for a competitor
  unchanged, it is not a work sample, it is a brochure.

## What not to do

- Do not offer to help. Do the work and attach it.
- Do not send a deck. Nobody opens a deck from a stranger.
- Do not audit their whole company. One problem, gone into properly.
- Do not claim access she does not have. "From the outside I can only see X, so
  I have assumed Y" is a strength, not a hedge.
- Do not reuse a sample across companies. The knowledge base tracks angles, not
  artifacts, for exactly this reason.
