# Work she could take alongside a full-time job

Queued deliverable, done 2026-08-26.

## The filter that matters

She left a lane deliberately. Work-life balance is a hard requirement, no
routinely working past 9pm IST. So the test is not "can she do this", it is
"does this hold a deadline over her evenings".

That rules out most of what gets called a side hustle: client service, anything
retainer-based with a response-time expectation, tutoring at fixed evening slots.
What survives is **async, project-shaped, and capped**.

**Moonlighting is cleared.** She confirmed on 2026-08-26 that Bounce is fine with
it. So the only remaining limits are her own time and the conflict rule at the
bottom of this file: nothing in EV mobility, gig platforms or rider services,
because that is a live conflict with her employer regardless of permission.

## The three worth doing

### 1. Paid writing

She has published in The Daily Brief at Zerodha. That is a real masthead and most
freelance pitches do not have one.

Business, consumer and markets explainers for newsletters and publications.
Async by nature, deadlines are weekly not hourly, and every piece compounds into
the portfolio she is already using in cold emails. The pet market piece is
carrying three of the seven approaches sent this week.

Pay is modest per piece. The reason to do it is not the money.

### 2. Market sizing and research projects

Her sizing method is unusual: a capture rate derived from live operating data
with the breaking assumption named. Most decks do not do that.

Small startups and micro-VCs buy this as one-off project work. Scoped, delivered,
done. No ongoing obligation.

This is also the most direct rehearsal for the thing she says she needs to learn,
which is whether customers will actually pay.

### 3. Agent and automation builds

She ships working agents, publicly. Small businesses want automations and cannot
hire for it.

Project-shaped, priced per build, and it keeps the technical half of her profile
current rather than decaying. This job agent is itself the portfolio piece.

## The one that is not paid but is worth more than the paid ones

**Bangalore Cat Squad.** She already fosters. Formalising something there, foster
operations or content, would be unpaid and would still be the highest-leverage
thing on this list.

Every pet-care approach she has sent leans on being a cat parent who fosters and
who found the vet system wanting. That credibility is the whole pitch. Deepening
it is not a side hustle, it is investment in the main search.

## What to avoid

- Anything with a response-time expectation. That is the thing she left.
- Evening-slot tutoring or mentoring. It eats exactly the hours she protects.
- Equity-only advisory for strangers. She has too little time to spend it on
  lottery tickets.
- Anything in EV mobility, gig platforms or rider services, which is a live
  conflict with her employer.
