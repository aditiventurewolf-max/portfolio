# Beyond School, and what the education search is actually for

Found in her Notion on 2026-08-26. This changes the education track completely,
and it is why she said to wait on Rocket Learning.

**She is not looking for a job in education. She has a venture concept and she is
looking for the apprenticeship that teaches her to build it.**

In her own words, from an MBA statement of purpose:

> "Over the past few months, I've been working on an alternative school model for
> high school students. The basic idea is to reduce the time students spend in
> traditional classrooms and give them space for project-based work and
> self-directed learning. Right now it's just a concept, but I'm serious about
> making it work."

And from a job application:

> "I want to develop something of my own, primarily focusing on the alternative
> education space. While I was trying to figure out the specifics of it, I
> realised that I need a little bit more experience about what exactly actually
> happens in the business space."

The venture has a name: **Beyond School**.

## What she already has worked out

This is not a daydream. The Notion work contains an ICP, a trust-building plan, a
GTM strategy and a funding map.

**The customer.** Parents who are annoyed with the system, understand why it
matters, and have money. Children who are bright but not thriving in a normal
school. She is explicit that the first 40 have to be personally convinced.

**How trust gets built**, since nobody enrols a child in an unproven school: the
IIM brand, a one-day trial experience, a two-week immersion, professors from
serious institutions, an affiliation with an established board, and deliberately
targeting parents who have already voiced dissent publicly.

**The model.** Flexible on curriculum and years. Learn by doing rather than
theory first. A few short classes plus a mentor check-in, then time, which she
identifies as the thing the current system never gives students. Weekly or
monthly showcases of student work. One-day job shadowing where the understanding
matters and not the number of days. Animals in the school. Caring for a plant.
Teaching boundaries, empathy, how to talk to service workers.

**The stated goals**: build a sense of self and identity, find passion points
rather than producing cogs, and teach decision-making and how to learn.

**The distribution idea, which is the sharpest part.** A premium journal called
**"The Source"**, positioned as the definitive voice on the future of learning in
India. Long-form essays, not listicles. Interviews with the unusual suspects, the
neuroscientist and the game designer and the ethicist rather than schoolteachers.
Her reasoning: "It positions us as architects, not salespeople" and "the most
influential parents are readers, not clickers." Alongside it, **Public Learning
Challenges** such as a "Delhi NCR 2040" project where multi-age teams work a real
city problem for four weeks and present to actual experts.

**Funding and space** she has already identified: Azim Premji Foundation,
Krishnamurti Foundation India (vvstudy@kfionline.org), Tata Trusts
(talktous@tatatrusts.org), Sri Aurobindo Foundation, corporate CSR for premises.

## The reading behind it

Tagore on being robbed of the earth to be taught geography. Ivan Illich,
*Deschooling Society*. John Holt, *How Children Fail* and *How Children Learn*.
*Letter to a Teacher*, School of Barbiana. Paulo Freire, *Pedagogy of the
Oppressed*. Gijubhai's *Divaswapna*. Krishnamurti. Mirambika Free Progress
School. Neel Bagh. Sylvia Ashton-Warner's key vocabulary method.

She has done the reading. Do not send her introductory material about alternative
education.

## What this means for targeting

The education search is a **means, not an end**. The right target is somewhere
she learns the specific things she has said she is missing: whether parents will
actually pay, how to manage money when it gets tight, how to hire people who can
execute, and what makes people trust a new idea.

So rank education targets by what they would teach her, not by mission alignment
alone:

- **Best:** an early-stage education company still proving parent willingness to
  pay, where she would sit close to the founder and see pricing, enrolment and
  retention decisions being made.
- **Good:** any 0-1 consumer business with a high-trust, high-consideration
  purchase, education or not. The trust problem is the transferable one.
- **Weak:** a large, well-funded education org where she would run one function
  well and learn none of the above. This is why Rocket Learning was the wrong
  first shot, however good the mission.
